MAIN RESULTS AND CONVENTIONS

A fusion matrix N_X is REGULAR left multiplication by [X] on
K_0(C) tensor C, in the basis of simple isomorphism classes. It is not a
selected small representation of that ring. Categories below are actual
unitary group-theoretical fusion categories, not hypothetical fusion rings.
They need not be braided. Their Drinfeld centers are modular, but no
noncyclotomic modular-data or symbol-field claim is made.

Theorem A (graph realization, with bounds).
Let Gamma be a finite simple undirected graph with n>=1 vertices, e edges
and maximum degree Delta. Put
 d=max(2,Delta),       N=4dn-4e.
There exist a unitary group-theoretical fusion category C, a self-dual
SIMPLE X in C, and a dominant tensor functor C -> Vec_(S_N), such that

 FPdim(X)=d,      FPdim(C)=d(N!)^d,
 rank(C)<=d(N!)^d<=d((4dn)!)^d,
 characteristic_polynomial(A_Gamma) divides
 characteristic_polynomial(N_X) in Z[t].

The self-duality is supplied by an explicit symmetric invariant pairing.
In particular no multiplicative scaling of the graph spectrum is needed.

Theorem B (exact arithmetic universality).
The union of spectra of self-dual simple objects in unitary
GROUP-THEORETICAL fusion categories is EXACTLY the set of totally real
algebraic integers. In particular every totally real algebraic integer
alpha occurs with scale m=1 in the REGULAR fusion matrix of ONE such
simple object.

The quantitative form in Theorem A takes any finite graph realization as
input. Section 6 additionally gives an explicit coefficient-input size and
positive-integer scaling bound from a minimal polynomial; it does not
assert a degree/height bound for the unscaled Salez tree realization.

Theorem C (a sharp integral-dimension threshold).
All eigenvalues of a self-dual simple object of FP dimension at most 2
in any complex fusion category are cyclotomic integers. There is a
self-dual simple of FP dimension 3 in an actual unitary group-theoretical
category with noncyclotomic fusion eigenvalues. Thus 3 is the least
INTEGER FP dimension permitting this phenomenon in integral categories.
No assertion about the interval 2<FPdim(X)<3 is made.

These statements resolve Liu--Palcoux--Ren Question 2.12 negatively, with
an exact universal realization theorem. The published graph realization,
equivariantization classification, Morita theory, and cyclotomic
character-TRACE theorem are all PRIOR. The new categorical interface is
the evaluated universal consequence of the cyclic lift below. The known
forgetful orbit-sum formula itself is also prior (Witherspoon Theorem 4.8).

1. THE CYCLIC SIMPLE LIFT AND AN ACTUAL TENSOR FUNCTOR

Proposition 1.
Let G be a finite group and s_0,...,s_(d-1) be DISTINCT elements satisfying
s_i^2=1, where d>=2. They need not generate G. There is a unitary
group-theoretical fusion category C and a self-dual simple X such that

 FPdim(C)=d |G|^d,       FPdim(X)=d,
 K_0(C) -> Z[G] is a SURJECTIVE UNITAL RING HOMOMORPHISM,
 [X] maps to a=s_0+...+s_(d-1).

The ring map is induced by a dominant tensor functor C -> Vec_G. Hence
characteristic_polynomial(L_a on C[G]) divides
characteristic_polynomial(N_X) in Z[t].

Construction and proof.
Write K=G^d and H=C_d=Z/d. Indices are in Z/d; let sigma_r act on K by
(sigma_r(g))_i=g_(i-r). This is a strict action by group automorphisms,
hence by unitary tensor autoequivalences rho_r on Vec_K. Define

                   C=(Vec_(G^d))^(C_d).

Concretely an object is a K-graded Hilbert space with unitary equivariance
u_r:rho_r(V)->V satisfying u_r rho_r(u_s)=u_(r+s). Tensor product is the
ordinary graded tensor product with diagonal equivariance. Morphisms,
adjoints, rigidity, and the positive pivotal structure are the usual ones.
This is the standard unitary equivariantization, of FP dimension
|H| FPdim(Vec_K)=d |G|^d.

Let t=(s_0,...,s_(d-1)) and O={sigma_r(t):r in H}. Distinctness of all
coordinates makes this cyclic orbit free, so |O|=d. Let X have one line
C e_g in each grade g in O. Define u_r(rho_r(e_g))=e_(sigma_r(g)).
These permutation maps satisfy the displayed equivariance equation, so
this specifies an ACTUAL equivariant object, with no projective
stabilizer ambiguity. A graded endomorphism is diagonal on its d distinct
grades; equivariance makes its d scalars equal. Thus End_C(X)=C, and X
is simple. Its underlying Hilbert-space dimension, and FP dimension, is d.
This also directly verifies the special orbit/inertia classification used
here; we are not assigning simplicity to an unchosen orbit sum.

Every g in O satisfies g^(-1)=g, because all its coordinates square to 1.
The pairing
                   b(e_g,e_h)=1 if g=h, and 0 otherwise
is a morphism X tensor X -> 1 in Vec_K: a nonzero pairing has grade
gh=g^2=1. It is H-invariant because all u_r are the same permutation on
both factors. It is symmetric and nondegenerate, with coevaluation
sum_g e_g tensor e_g. The two zigzags hold on this displayed basis. It
therefore gives the required EQUIVARIANT self-duality, not merely a
self-dual forgetful object. The associated map X->X* is unitary. With
the standard pivotal structure the same pairing gives second indicator +1,
although that indicator is not needed for the spectral conclusion.

The following orbit-sum forgetful formula is a special case of PRIOR
Witherspoon Theorem 4.8. We give the exact maps to establish the full
regular quotient needed here. The forgetful tensor functor C->Vec_K
followed by push-forward of grading
along the group homomorphism p_1:K->G is a tensor functor
                         T:C->Vec_G.
Push-forward takes a line in grade (g_0,...,g_(d-1)) to grade g_0; since
p_1(gh)=p_1(g)p_1(h), its tensor constraints are the canonical ones. Thus
its Grothendieck map psi is a unital ring homomorphism. Each s_i occurs
exactly once as the first coordinate of the d orbit tuples, giving
                            psi([X])=sum_i s_i.

For g != 1 in G, apply the identical free-orbit construction to the tuple
(g,1,...,1); it has d distinct orbit points even though some coordinates
repeat. Call this simple Y_g. Then
                psi([Y_g])=g+(d-1)1,       psi([1_C])=1.
Consequently every group basis element is in the Z-linear image:
g=psi([Y_g]-(d-1)[1_C]) for g!=1. Hence psi:K_0(C)->Z[G] is SURJECTIVE,
and T is dominant since every graded simple occurs in some T(Y_g).

The kernel of psi is stable under left multiplication by [X]. Its quotient
is the FREE abelian group Z[G], with left multiplication by a. A basis
adapted to this integral quotient, or ordinary characteristic polynomial
factorization on its complexification, gives the asserted divisibility
in Z[t]. This is a quotient of the REGULAR fusion representation; no
identification with a full matrix algebra is needed.

Finally the standard equivariantization/crossed-product Morita theorem
identifies C up to Morita equivalence with Vec_K crossed H. Because the
action is strict, this crossed product is the pointed category on the
semidirect product G^d semidirect C_d, with multiplication
       (g,r)(h,s)=(g sigma_r(h),r+s).
Thus C is group-theoretical. This invokes the established theorem stated
in Mombelli--Natale Example 7.5/Remark 7.6, not a new Morita result.
All dimensions above are exact. This completes Proposition 1.

2. QUANTITATIVE GRAPH INPUT (GODSIL'S CONSTRUCTION, RESTATED)

This section is supporting PRIOR graph theory. We restate the relevant
construction to fix its size and multiplicity bounds explicitly. It is
Godsil's regularization/matching route, not a new graph realization theorem.

Let d=max(2,Delta). For each vertex v of Gamma form d-deg(v) deficit
labels (v,j). Their number is delta=dn-2e>=0. Construct a graph Y from
d disjoint copies of Gamma, with vertices (i,v), and the delta deficit
vertices. Join (v,j) to (i,v) for EVERY copy i. Retain the original edges
within each copy. This is simple and undirected. Each copy vertex now
has d neighbors; each deficit vertex has exactly d neighbors. Thus Y is
d-regular on
                          M=dn+delta=2dn-2e
vertices. The map
                 x -> (0,x,-x,0,...,0)
from the original n-dimensional adjacency module to the deficit/d-copy
coordinates intertwines A_Gamma and A_Y. It is injective because d>=2.
Therefore charpoly(A_Gamma) divides charpoly(A_Y).

The bipartite double has adjacency
                         B = [ 0  A_Y ; A_Y  0 ]
and N=2M=4dn-4e vertices. The diagonal subspace y->(y,y) intertwines
A_Y and B. The double is d-regular bipartite. Hall's theorem, iterated
after removing a matching, partitions its edges into d perfect matchings.
Their adjacency matrices are the permutation matrices of d DISTINCT
fixed-point-free involutions s_0,...,s_(d-1) in G=S_N, and their sum is B.

We choose FULL G=S_N, rather than necessarily the subgroup generated by
the matchings. This ensures the natural N-dimensional permutation module
embeds in the regular group module, WITH multiplicities. Explicitly let
H_0=S_(N-1) be the stabilizer of vertex 1. The normalized sums of group
basis vectors in the N left cosets g H_0 form an invariant subspace of
C[G], permuted exactly as those N vertices. Thus B is the restriction of
L_a, a=sum_i s_i, to that subspace, and
        charpoly(A_Gamma) | charpoly(A_Y) | charpoly(B) | charpoly(L_a).
All these are integral monic polynomials. No claim of connectedness of
the resulting Cayley graph is needed.

Apply Proposition 1 to G=S_N and the d matchings. Its regular quotient
then proves Theorem A. The rank bound follows from rank(C)<=FPdim(C),
as every simple has FP dimension at least 1. The construction is finite
and explicit, using a perfect-matching decomposition, and has no hidden
categorification hypothesis.

3. EXACT UNIVERSALITY, NECESSITY, AND THE PRIOR ARITHMETIC QUESTION

Salez Theorem 1 states that every totally real algebraic integer alpha is
an eigenvalue of a finite tree. Apply Theorem A to that tree. This proves
the sufficiency in Theorem B with m=1.

Conversely, by rigidity N_X^transpose=N_(X*). A self-dual X therefore has
a symmetric integral fusion matrix. Its characteristic polynomial is a
real-rooted monic integral polynomial, so every eigenvalue is a totally
real algebraic integer. This proves necessity, hence the EXACT equality
of the two sets in Theorem B.

Liu--Palcoux--Ren Theorem 2.11 proves cyclotomicity of fusion-matrix
eigenvalues in a COMMUTATIVE categorifiable fusion ring, and Question 2.12
asks whether the criterion extends to the noncommutative case. Taking
any noncyclotomic totally real algebraic integer in Theorem B disproves
that extension. The example below supplies an explicit one in a simple
of FP dimension 3.

ENO Theorem 8.51 gives cyclotomic character traces, and Corollary 8.53
provides cyclotomic models of irreducible representations. Those PRIOR
results are consistent with everything above. An individual eigenvalue
of a higher-dimensional matrix need not belong to its coefficient field.
In particular the theorem does not assert noncyclotomic F-symbol fields
or a failure of modular-data cyclotomicity. The categories constructed
are group-theoretical. The word SIMPLE refers to X, not to the algebraic
multiplicity of one eigenvalue. Noncyclotomic regular eigenvalues must
repeat in higher-dimensional regular blocks, consistent with the prior
Calegari--Morrison--Snyder multiplicity-one observation.

4. EXPLICIT NONCYCLOTOMIC CONTROL AT SIMPLE DIMENSION THREE

Let
 G=<r,s | r^10=s^2=1, srs=r^(-1)>,       |G|=20,
and take the three distinct involutions s,rs,r^3 s. Proposition 1 gives
 C=(Vec_(G^3))^(C_3),         FPdim(C)=24000,
and a self-dual simple X of FP dimension 3, with
                         psi([X])=a=s+rs+r^3s.

Let zeta=exp(pi i/5). In the two-dimensional representation
 pi(r)=diag(zeta,zeta^(-1)),      pi(s)=[0 1;1 0],
pi(a) has off-diagonal entries u=1+zeta+zeta^3 and its conjugate. Thus
its eigenvalues are +/-lambda, where
 lambda^2=|u|^2
  =3+(zeta+zeta^-1)+(zeta^2+zeta^-2)+(zeta^3+zeta^-3)
  =(7+sqrt(5))/2.

Set f(t)=t^4-7t^2+11. This is irreducible: y=(7+sqrt(5))/2 has degree 2,
and is not a square in Q(sqrt(5)), because its field norm is 11, not a
rational square. Hence lambda has degree 4.

Moreover Q(lambda) is NOT Galois over Q. It contains Q(sqrt(5)). If it
contained sqrt(11), it would equal the degree-four biquadratic field
Q(sqrt(5),sqrt(11)). Write lambda=A+B sqrt(11), A,B in Q(sqrt(5)). Since
lambda^2=y is in that quadratic field, AB=0. If B=0 then y is a square
there, impossible by its norm 11. If A=0 then y/11 is a square there,
impossible by its norm 1/11. Thus sqrt(11) is absent. The conjugate
sqrt((7-sqrt(5))/2)=sqrt(11)/lambda is consequently absent, proving
non-normality. Every subfield of a cyclotomic field is Galois over Q,
so lambda is NONCYCLOTOMIC. It occurs in the regular group spectrum,
and hence in N_X by Proposition 1.

For an exact small verification, the regular group matrix L_a has
characteristic polynomial
 (t^2-9)(t^2-1)(t^4-7t^2+11)^2(t^4-3t^2+1)^2.
In particular the noncyclotomic eigenvalues already repeat there.
The category has rank 2720: among the 20^3 graded tuples, 20 constant
tuples have inertia C_3 and give three one-dimensional simples each;
the other 7980 form 2660 free orbits and give three-dimensional simples.
Thus 60+2660=2720 and 60+9*2660=24000. This is an actual category,
not a putative matrix. No 2720-by-2720 fusion matrix is generated or
needed; the exact 20-by-20 group calculation checks the stated control.

5. THE SHARP INTEGRAL-DIMENSION THRESHOLD

Let X be self-dual with FPdim(X)<=2 in any complex fusion category.
Its symmetric integral matrix has spectral radius FPdim(X), so every
conjugate of every eigenvalue beta is in [-2,2]. If z satisfies
z^2-beta z+1=0, it is an algebraic integer and all its conjugates have
absolute value 1. Kronecker's theorem makes z a root of unity. Hence
beta=z+z^(-1) is cyclotomic. The preceding example has FPdim(X)=3,
proving Theorem C. This is a consequence of the complete construction,
not a separate intended paper or a claimed classification of fusion
categories generated by dimension-three objects.

6. EXPLICIT COEFFICIENT-INPUT BOUNDS FOR THE SCALED TARGET

For completeness the original positive-integer-scaled target can be
made effective directly in terms of a polynomial, with deliberately
coarse bounds. This supporting linear algebra does not replace the
stronger unscaled universality from Salez.

Suppose alpha has monic irreducible totally real minimal polynomial of
degree r>=1 and integer coefficient height H>=1. Put R=H+1 and
L=r R^(2r-2). Let M be multiplication by alpha on the power basis of
Q(alpha), and let Q_ij=Tr(alpha^(i+j)), 0<=i,j<r. Then Q is positive
definite integral, QM=M^transpose Q, and every entry has absolute value
at most L. Write Delta_i for its positive leading principal minors,
Delta_0=1, and P=product_(i=1)^r Delta_i. Define
                          ell=P^5,       kappa=ell R.
In particular
 ell <= (product_(i=1)^r i!)^5 L^(5r(r+1)/2).

Here is a completely rational symmetric realization with controlled
denominators. In Q=L_0 D L_0^transpose, take L_0 unit lower triangular.
Its entries have denominators dividing P, by the usual minor formula
for rational LDL decomposition. Write D_i=a_i/b_i in lowest terms;
b_i divides Delta_(i-1), hence P. Express the positive integer a_i b_i
as four integer squares (Lagrange's theorem). Dividing those four
integers by b_i produces a column block whose squared norm is D_i.
The resulting 4r-by-r matrix B_0 has B_0^transpose B_0=D and denominator
dividing P. Set B=B_0 L_0^transpose. Then
               B^transpose B=Q,       denominator(B) divides P^2.
Consequently
                    A=B M Q^(-1) B^transpose
is symmetric rational, AB=BM, and is zero on the orthogonal complement
of im(B). Its eigenvalues are exactly the conjugates of alpha, plus 3r
zeros. Since the denominator of Q^(-1) divides Delta_r, that of A
divides P^5. Thus W=ell A is a symmetric INTEGER matrix of size 4r.
All its eigenvalues have absolute value at most ell R=kappa, so every
entry of W has absolute value at most kappa.

We now use the block construction in Godsil Lemma 2.4, keeping an explicit
size bound. For any two matrices U,V the block matrix
                 C(U,V)=[U V; V U]
has invariant diagonal and anti-diagonal subspaces with restrictions
U+V and U-V. First write W=W_+-W_- and pass to C(W_+,W_-), a nonnegative
symmetric integral matrix of twice the size. Next split its off-diagonal
part U and diagonal part V and pass to C(U,V). The result has ZERO
diagonal, is symmetric nonnegative integral, and retains the entire
spectrum of W. If an entry exceeds 1, split the current matrix as D+E,
where E is its support indicator and D subtracts 1 from each positive
entry, and replace it by C(D,E). Each iteration reduces the maximal
entry by one, keeping zero diagonal and symmetry. At most kappa-1
iterations produce the adjacency of a simple graph Gamma. Therefore

 n_Gamma <= 2^(kappa+3) r,       Delta_Gamma <= 4r kappa.

The degree bound follows because the row sums after the first block
step are the absolute row sums of W, at most 4r kappa, and every
subsequent block step preserves row sums. These are classical graph
spectral operations; no new graph theorem is asserted.

Apply Theorem A to this Gamma. With D=max(2,Delta_Gamma), one obtains
ell alpha in the REGULAR matrix of one self-dual simple, with bounds

 FPdim(X)=D <= 4r kappa,
 N <= 2^(kappa+7) r^2 kappa,
 FPdim(C) <= (4r kappa) ((2^(kappa+7) r^2 kappa)!)^(4r kappa).

This gives explicit scale and size bounds from the polynomial input.
They are intentionally enormous; no efficient recognition algorithm,
optimal degree-height bound or unscaled coefficient-size bound is claimed.
All operations are finite exact constructions. Theoretical bounds do not
require running these huge constructions on the group's server.
